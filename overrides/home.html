A complete research system
for neuroscience teams.

Nineteen phases from the first question to the published paper, and on to the next. Memory, tasks and deadlines for you, the project and the team — plain files, inside Claude Code.

01 / 08 — QUESTION
It starts with a question. The literature is searched and read with you; hypotheses and the analysis plan are frozen before any data exist.
02 / 08 — FUNDING
The grant and the budget are written from the same record as the plan, so all three say the same thing.
03 / 08 — INSTRUMENTS
Paradigms are audited for timing before anyone sits down. Analysis tools are built for their job and checked against known answers.
04 / 08 — DATA
Recordings land in BIDS and raw stays raw. Every preprocessing step is on record; confirmatory tests run on the frozen plan.
05 / 08 — MODELS
Computational models pass a smoke test before the first long run. Parameter searches and cluster jobs are registered and tracked.
06 / 08 — WRITING
Paper, reviews, poster and reports are written against the project's own record, then read sentence by sentence.
07 / 08 — SHARING
Exports and archives leave with local files held back. Notes taken along the way become the next question.
08 / 08 — MEMORY · YOU
Your profile and writing style, your ideas, a board for your own tasks and a personal wiki — in a private repository that follows you from project to project.
08 / 08 — MEMORY · PROJECT
Each project keeps one memory in its own git: objectives, deadlines, a task board, meetings that turn into tasks, and every decision with its reason.
08 / 08 — MEMORY · TEAM
A hive connects the lab's projects: members, research directions, a team board and a shared wiki. Nothing personal leaves without a yes.
THE NEXT STUDY
The end of one study is the start of the next — and it begins with everything you, the project and the team have learned.
